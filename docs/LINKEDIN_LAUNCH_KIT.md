## LinkedIn post draft

I built **King of the Court**, an interactive mobile prototype for helping basketball players find nearby games, organize squads, and compete for recognition at their local courts.

The idea came from a simple problem: pickup basketball still depends heavily on scattered group chats, uncertain court activity, and being in the right place at the right time.

The prototype includes:

- 1v1 and team-game discovery
- 2v2 through 5v5 squad building
- Player ratings and competitive profiles
- Live court-activity concepts
- Court-specific top-player rankings
- A “King of the Court” challenge mechanic

I built the experience with **React Native, Expo, and TypeScript**, using reusable components and typed domain models for players, courts, games, and squads.

This is currently a front-end product prototype using representative data. My next milestone is persistent game flows, followed by authentication, privacy-conscious court check-ins, and verified results.

I would love feedback from basketball players, mobile developers, and anyone who has worked on local marketplace products.

## Portfolio page copy

### One-line summary

A mobile basketball platform connecting nearby players, squads, courts, and competitive reputation.

### Project description

King of the Court explores how a mobile product can make local pickup basketball easier to organize and more rewarding to return to. Players can discover 1v1 or team games, invite friends into squads, inspect nearby-court activity, and build a competitive identity through ratings and court-specific leaderboards.

The current interactive prototype was built in React Native and TypeScript with an intentionally replaceable data layer. The product blueprint extends the concept into verified results, privacy-conscious check-ins, geographic matchmaking, and court crown challenges.

## Interview talking points

Be ready to explain:

1. Why court-level check-ins are safer than exposing live player coordinates.
2. Why ratings must be calculated on a trusted backend.
3. How repeat-opponent farming and disputed results could be handled.
4. Why the first market should concentrate on a few courts instead of launching everywhere.
5. How the current mock-data layer would be replaced without rewriting the screens.
6. Which parts are implemented today and which belong to the product roadmap.
